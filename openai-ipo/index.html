05

MY $25,000 / THE HONEST CASE

CURRENT STANCE: WAIT FOR THE TERMS

My hypothetical $25K stays uninvested until the evidence supports a price. Interest in owning OpenAI is not a commitment to spend the whole budget.

THE CASE FOR IT

ChatGPT, Codex and the API offer several routes to paid demand. In March, OpenAI reported $2B monthly revenue. That establishes commercial scale at that time—not current profit or a guaranteed growth rate. [3]

WHAT COULD GO WRONG

Competition and price cuts could limit margins while computing costs stay high. New funding and employee equity could dilute ownership. The Foundation controls the business. I need the public financials and offering terms to price those risks. [4]

No OpenAI offer price is assumed. This only illustrates whole-share arithmetic for $25,000.

06

WHAT I WOULD DO NEXT

  1. Read the public filing before setting a price. Check actual revenue, cash flow, compute obligations, governance and dilution. Then judge what growth the valuation already assumes.
  2. Decide how much deserves to be invested. $25K is the available hypothetical budget, not an amount that must be spent. A personal recommendation would need the investor's wider finances.
  3. If the investment passes, choose an entry plan.

    01SINGLE LIMIT

    One purchase at or below a justified price.
    + Simple
    − One entry point; may not fill

    02LIMIT LADDER

    Split the chosen amount across price limits.
    + Controls entry prices
    − Several orders can fill together

    03STAGED BUYING

    Five planned installments, if the thesis holds.
    + Spreads entry timing
    − May pay more if shares rise
    My conditional preference: five equal portions over five weeks. If all $25K were justified, that is $5K each. With a price ceiling, a scheduled purchase may be skipped; that is not pure DCA. [5] [6]
  4. Keep researching. Be willing to change my mind. Review on a public filing, pricing announcement, confirmed broker access or material financial news. Staged buying does not protect against a failed business. Neither does refusing to sell at a loss.
