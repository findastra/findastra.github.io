Claude makes Anthropic worth studying. It does not make every entry price attractive. I would wait for final pricing, test the valuation, then consider a small position. Missing an opening-day jump is an acceptable outcome.

Company-reported Q3 figures. Adjusted margin is non-GAAP; it is not the same as reported operating profit. [1]

WHY I WOULD CONSIDER OWNING IT

The revenue scale and growth support a serious commercial thesis. Claude's coding and enterprise uses could produce recurring demand. If revenue outgrows the cost of serving it, operating leverage could improve earnings. That last step is my thesis to test, not a reported result.

WHY I WOULD KEEP THE POSITION SMALL

Competition can compress prices. Compute capacity can tie up cash before demand arrives. Growth can disappoint while the valuation falls. Employee equity and financing can dilute owners; public shareholders do not control the company. The Class F vote carries 50.1% of general voting power. [2]

Read losses carefully. Q3 net loss was $23.3B, including a $21.9B non-cash fair-value charge. That is not $23.3B of quarterly cash burn. [1] Conversely, nine-month operating cash flow of +$6.569B is not free cash flow or proof that future infrastructure needs are funded. [2]

THE PRICE / WHAT SUCCESS IS ALREADY PRICED IN?

ONE REPRODUCIBLE REFERENCE POINT

$74 × 13.0867B shares ≈ $968.4B
$17B quarterly revenue × 4 = $68B
$968.4B ÷ $68B ≈ 14.2× annualized sales

Derived arithmetic, not a fair-value estimate. The filing gives 13,086,672,593 post-offering shares at its $74 midpoint assumptions, including the private placement and without the underwriters' extra-share option. This is not fully diluted: options, unsettled RSUs and other potential issuances are excluded. Terms and share counts can change with pricing. [2] Annualizing Q3 does not forecast a full year. [1]

My reading: this price asks for substantial future success. At a purely illustrative 20% mature net margin, $68B of annual revenue would produce $13.6B of profit; $968.4B would be about 71× that hypothetical profit. That is a demanding starting point even before adding dilution. A 20% net margin is an assumption, not Anthropic's current margin.

A headline valuation is incomplete without its share-count definition. I would reconcile basic, diluted and future reserved shares before using a $1T or $1.4T headline to set my limit.

$75 is an illustration, not a quote or my buy recommendation. Whole shares from a hypothetical $25,000.

05

OPTION 3 / USEFUL, WITH LIMITS

I prefer staged entry if the valuation passes. It spreads the risk of picking one entry date and gives me time to learn. It does not avoid day-one exposure if I buy on day one, and it cannot rescue a bad investment. When prices rise, delaying purchases can mean fewer shares and a lower return. [6]

Set a maximum price for the chosen amount.
+ Simple price discipline

02 LIMIT LADDER

Divide money among several price limits.
+ Planned prices

03 STAGED ENTRY

Reassess before each planned purchase.
+ Time to learn
− May miss gains or add to losses

Pure dollar-cost averaging invests equal amounts at regular intervals regardless of price. My version has valuation and thesis checks, so it is conditional staged buying. [7]

SAME $25K / DIFFERENT PRICE PATHS

A teaching model, not a forecast or backtest. Five weekly observations; every strategy starts with $25K. Values are compared immediately after week 5.

Strategy comparison, including unspent cash
StrategySharesCash leftFinal total

Rules: single limit: $25K at a $90 cap; ladder: one-third each at $90 / $80 / $75; scheduled: up to $5K each week, no price cap. Limits remain eligible through week 5. At each observed price, every eligible order fills in full at that price. Whole shares only; each installment's leftover stays cash. These $90/$80/$75 levels are teaching examples, not recommended limits.

Real fills depend on liquidity, timing and broker rules; between-observation moves are ignored. Final total = shares × week-5 price + cash. No taxes, fees, dividends or cash interest. Holding cash has an opportunity cost. The scheduled strategy is a comparison baseline, not my conditional plan.

  1. Wait for final terms and at least one full trading session. Reconcile the price and share count, then build a range of earnings outcomes. The $70–$78 filing range is not my independent estimate of fair value.
  2. Only then consider a starter of up to $5K. Use a limit justified by that analysis. This cap is my hypothetical risk budget, not a finding that $5K suits someone else's portfolio. If $25K were all my investable savings, I would not commit it all to one IPO.
  3. Keep at least $20K in reserve after that starter. Revisit weekly for four weeks; add up to $5K at a review only if the valuation and business case still work. Skipped purchases stay cash. An IPO allocation, if any, counts toward the total position cap.
  4. Stop adding when the evidence weakens. Watch customer retention, margins, compute needs and dilution. Read the staged share-release schedule before calling every dip a bargain. [2] I would reduce or exit on a broken thesis, not promise never to sell at a loss.
